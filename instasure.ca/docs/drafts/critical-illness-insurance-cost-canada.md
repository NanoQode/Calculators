Your critical illness insurance cost depends mostly on your age, whether you smoke and how much coverage you buy. For $100,000 of 10-year coverage, Instasure's model estimates about $52 to $55 a month for a 35-year-old non-smoker, as of October 2026. Below are dated tables by age, sex, smoking status and amount, plus the choices that move your own price.

## How much does critical illness insurance cost in Canada?

For $100,000 of 10-year comprehensive coverage, Instasure's model estimates about $55 a month for a 35-year-old female non-smoker ($45 to $67) and $52 for a male non-smoker ($42.50 to $63), as of October 2026. A 45-year-old pays about $83.50 (female) or $97 (male). Smokers pay close to double.

These are Instasure estimates for example profiles, not quotes or averages. Real quotes can be noticeably lower or higher, because each insurer prices the same profile its own way. Use the tables to see how the price moves, then get an [instant critical illness estimate](/critical-illness-insurance/) for your own age and amount.

What are you buying? The [Canadian Life and Health Insurance Association (CLHIA)](https://www.clhia.ca/en-ca/media-and-publications/news-releases/2024/got-questions-about-critical-illness-insurance-clhias-latest-consumer-guide-has-answers-to-help-you) describes a non-taxable lump sum paid after a diagnosis such as cancer, heart attack, stroke, multiple sclerosis or Parkinson's disease.

## What drives critical illness insurance rates in Canada?

In our model, four things do most of the work: age, smoking, the amount and the plan. Your health then shapes what an insurer actually offers.

### Age

Age matters most. From 40 on, the estimate for a male non-smoker roughly doubles every 10 years: $70 a month at 40, $137 at 50 and $272 at 60 for $100,000. For a female non-smoker, it rises by about 60% to 100% each decade. In the model, women's estimates are slightly higher than men's up to age 44, and lower from 45.

### Smoking

The critical illness insurance price for smokers is close to double in our model: $102.50 a month for a 35-year-old female smoker with $100,000, against $55 for a non-smoker.

Each insurer defines "smoker" its own way. [Sun Life's critical illness underwriting](https://www.sunlife.ca/slfas/Resources/New+business+and+underwriting/CII+-+Underwriting/Smoker+definitions?vgnLocale=en_CA), for example, asks for 12 months without any nicotine product, including gum or patches. It treats occasional cannabis users as non-smokers, but other insurers may not. Answer the smoking questions accurately; a misstatement can put a future claim at risk.

### Coverage amount

What does a $100,000 critical illness insurance cost look like next to other amounts? For a 35-year-old female non-smoker, $50,000 is about $28.50 a month, $100,000 is $55 and $250,000 is $124. Price grows a little more slowly than coverage, because the model spreads a small policy fee over more coverage and uses a slightly lower rate per $1,000 from $250,000.

### Conditions covered

A basic plan covers cancer, heart attack and stroke. A comprehensive plan covers a longer list, and some pay a partial benefit for early-stage illness. CLHIA publishes [benchmark definitions for critical illnesses](https://www.clhia.ca/en-ca/Consumers/Consumer-Guides/Critical-Illness-Benchmark-Definitions), last reviewed in 2024. Insurers choose which definitions to use, so two policies with the same condition names can pay out differently.

### Your health

Our model assumes standard health. A medical condition or family history can change the price or the terms. Our [medical underwriting desk](/life-insurance/medical-conditions/) helps people in that position.

## Term 10 vs Term 75 vs Term 100: how the term changes the cost

How long the coverage lasts changes how you pay for it.

| Plan type | How the premium works | When coverage ends |
|---|---|---|
| Term 10 or Term 20 (renewable) | Level for 10 or 20 years, then renews at a higher premium set in the contract | At a set age, such as 75 |
| Term 65 or Term 75 (level) | Level until the plan ends; some insurers offer a 15-year payment option | At 65 or 75 |
| Term 100 (lifetime) | Level for life, paid over 10 or 15 years or to age 100 | Coverage for life |

*Plan features from [Sun Life's critical illness plans](https://www.sunlife.ca/en/health/critical-illness-insurance/sun-critical-illness-insurance/) and [Manulife's Lifecheque](https://advisor.manulife.ca/advisors/insurance/living-benefits/lifecheque.html). Other insurers offer different terms.*

Our model prices Term 10 only, so we don't show numbers for Term 75 or Term 100. A level premium starts higher than a Term 10 premium at the same age, but it doesn't jump at renewal.

What we can show is how fast a new 10-year plan gets more expensive with age. For a female non-smoker with $100,000, our model gives $55 a month at 35, $83.50 at 45, $165.50 at 55 and $311.50 at 65. Your Term 10 renewal premiums are set in your contract and won't match these figures, but they climb with age too.

So don't compare this year's premiums alone. If you'll want coverage into your 60s or 70s, add up the Term 10 renewals before you compare them with a level plan.

## What return of premium adds to the cost

A return of premium rider refunds some or all of the premiums you paid if you never claim, under the rider's conditions. Insurers offer different versions. Manulife's Lifecheque, for example, lists return of premium on death, at expiry and with an early surrender option. The share you get back depends on the rider's schedule and how long you've held the policy.

## How to lower your critical illness premium

You can't change your age, but most other levers are yours:

1. **Buy sooner.** Each year of delay moves you further down the tables above.
2. **Quit smoking.** Once you meet your insurer's nicotine-free period, ask whether your rate can be reviewed.
3. **Size the amount to a job,** such as months off work or treatment costs, not a round number.
4. **Choose the plan scope on purpose.** A basic plan costs less but covers far fewer conditions.
5. **Decide on return of premium separately.** Add it only if you'd keep the policy long enough for the refund to matter.
6. **Check your group benefits.** If your employer already provides critical illness coverage, you may need less on your own.
7. **Compare insurers.** Each one prices age, sex and health differently, and a licensed advisor can quote several.

## Is the cost worth it for you?

Start with the risk. In its 2026 Cancer Awareness Month message, the [federal Minister of Health](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/news/2026/08/message-from-the-minister-of-health--cancer-awareness-month.html) said about 2 in 5 people in Canada are expected to be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime. The same message projected about 254,100 new cancer diagnoses in 2026.

Then look at your cushion. Coverage matters most when a diagnosis would strain your finances: if you're self-employed, live on one income or have no group benefits. The lump sum can replace lost income or pay costs your provincial health plan doesn't cover.

Disability insurance replaces income month by month, while critical illness pays one lump sum. Our guide to [critical illness vs disability insurance](/guides/critical-illness-vs-disability-insurance/) shows how the two fit together. ## Michael's take

As a planner, I don't start with the premium. I start with the job the money has to do. A critical illness payout buys you time: time off work, time for a partner to be with you, time to recover without draining your savings. Size the coverage to that job, then look at the tables. The age tables make the timing point for me. If coverage belongs in your plan, every year you wait moves you to a higher starting premium.

Where I'd be careful is the extras. In our model, return of premium adds about 45% to the premium. If that extra would squeeze your RRSP or TFSA contributions, I'd rather keep the base plan and invest the difference. And I wouldn't let critical illness crowd out disability or life insurance when people depend on your income. Those protect the plan first.
