There's no official average for the car insurance Scarborough drivers pay, because no regulator publishes figures for Toronto districts. What we can give you is a dated Instasure estimate for Toronto, the regulator's figure for the Greater Toronto Area, and a clear view of how your postal code, record and car move the price. Then you can check your own number in about a minute.

## How much is car insurance in Scarborough?

For an example 40-year-old driver licensed 15+ years, with no tickets or claims, a standard vehicle and full coverage, Instasure estimates Toronto car insurance at $214 a month, or $171 to $266, as of October 2026. Our model applies the Toronto estimate to Scarborough. Your own quote can land higher or lower.

For context, the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA) publishes [average premiums by region](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/auto-insurance/understanding-auto-insurance-rates/your-average-premium). For the 12 months to October 2025, the average was $2,810 a year in the GTA, against $2,164 for Ontario as a whole. That puts the GTA about 30% above the provincial figure.

FSRA's averages cover every private passenger vehicle insured in Ontario over the period. They blend every driver, vehicle and coverage choice, so they don't line up exactly with one example driver.

## Why your Scarborough postal code matters

Insurers don't price Scarborough as one place. Each insurer groups postal codes into rating territories, and FSRA approves how those territories are defined and priced. Your address decides which territory, and which local claims record, your premium starts from.

For years, FSRA's [territory filing rules](https://www.fsrao.ca/media/24271/download) allowed no more than 55 territories across Ontario and 10 in the City of Toronto. That kept each Toronto territory large.

That's changing. Under FSRA's [Territories Test and Learn Environment](https://www.fsrao.ca/announcements/shaping-future-territory-rating-auto-insurance-through-innovation), participating insurers can redraw and reprice territories inside the GTA. The changes apply to policies effective on or after July 2, 2024, and the test runs for at least two years. So the gap between Scarborough neighbourhoods can now differ more from one insurer to the next.

## Why is car insurance expensive in Scarborough?

Scarborough is part of the GTA, which has the highest of FSRA's regional averages. FSRA explains that [rates are usually higher in urban areas](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/auto-insurance/understanding-auto-insurance-rates/what-determines-your-auto-insurance-rate), where accidents and car theft are more frequent. It adds that the likelihood and cost of collisions, theft and weather differ from one region to another.

Market-wide costs push the same way. FSRA's page on [how market conditions affect rates](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/auto-insurance/understanding-auto-insurance-rates/how-market-conditions-affect-rates) points to inflation in auto parts and labour, more drivers on the road and rising auto theft. Our guide on [why car insurance is going up in 2026](/guides/why-is-my-car-insurance-going-up-2026/) covers those trends in detail.

### Theft, collisions and commuting

- **Theft.** FSRA lists how likely your vehicle is to be stolen among the vehicle factors insurers weigh. The Toronto Police Service publishes auto theft counts by neighbourhood, with rates per 100,000 people, in its [Neighbourhood Crime Rates open data](https://data.tps.ca/datasets/TorontoPS::neighbourhood-crime-rates-open-data/about). We don't quote a Scarborough figure, because the police note the data is preliminary and locations are offset to the nearest intersection.
- **Collisions.** Collision claims in your territory, and what those repairs cost, feed into its rate. Your own accidents and tickets then carry a lot of weight on top.
- **Commuting.** FSRA says insurers consider the kilometres you drive each year and whether you drive to work or school. Usually, the more you drive, the higher your premium. If you've switched your commute to transit, tell your insurer.

## Ways Scarborough drivers lower premiums

There's no single trick for cheap car insurance in Scarborough. These steps work with how Ontario insurers actually rate you:

1. **Compare Scarborough car insurance quotes at every renewal.** FSRA notes that premiums differ from one insurer to another, and each insurer draws its own territories. Get fresh [car insurance quotes](/quote/car-insurance/) before you auto-renew.
2. **Protect your record.** In the model, one minor ticket adds about $39 a month and one at-fault claim about $75. Paying for a small repair yourself can sometimes cost less than the premium increase over several years.
3. **Price the car before you buy it.** Insurers weigh a vehicle's safety features, how likely it is to be stolen and how much it costs to repair.
4. **Set your deductible with your savings in mind.** FSRA explains that a lower deductible generally means a higher premium. Choose a deductible you could pay the week after a claim.
5. **Report your real driving.** Update your annual kilometres and commute whenever they change.
6. **Review accident benefits with care.** Since July 1, 2026, [FSRA confirms](https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/changes-statutory-accident-benefits-coverage-ontario-july-1-2026) that only medical, rehabilitation and attendant care benefits are mandatory. Income replacement, non-earner, caregiver and funeral benefits are among those now optional. Existing policies renew with the same coverage unless you choose to change it. Read our [Ontario auto insurance changes 2026](/guides/ontario-auto-insurance-changes-july-2026/) guide first.
7. **Bundle and ask about discounts.** A [home and auto bundle](/car-insurance/home-and-auto-bundle/) can help if you own or rent your home.

One thing you don't need to worry about: FSRA's [rating and underwriting guidance](https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/regulatory-framework/guidance-auto-insurance/automobile-insurance-rating-and-underwriting-guidance) says credit information can't be used as a factor in auto insurance rating or underwriting in Ontario.

## Michael's take

As a Toronto-based planner, I'd treat your postal code as the one rating factor you can't negotiate. What you do control is your record, your car and your coverage. My rule of thumb is to price the insurance before you choose the car, because the theft-risk gap in the table above adds up over the years you own it.

I'd also look at car insurance as part of the household budget, not a bill to shave at any cost. Set the deductible at an amount your emergency fund could cover tomorrow, and keep your liability limit high. On the July 2026 changes, price each optional benefit before you drop it. For a family that depends on a paycheque, income replacement can matter more than the few dollars a month it saves.
