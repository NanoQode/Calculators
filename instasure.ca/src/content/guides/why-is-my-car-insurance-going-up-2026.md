Why is my car insurance going up when my driving hasn't changed? In 2026, most increases come from industry-wide costs: pricier repairs, vehicle theft, injury claims and severe weather. Province-specific rules matter too, and your own profile still counts. The good news is that there are practical ways to push back.

## What is pushing car insurance premiums up across Canada?

Insurers set rates based on what they expect to pay in claims. When claim costs rise for drivers like you, premiums follow. Four pressures stand out.

### Repair costs

Modern vehicles are packed with technology. Bumpers, mirrors and windshields often hold cameras, radar or other sensors for driver-assistance features. A minor collision can mean replacing and recalibrating those parts, not just fixing a dent. Parts prices and labour costs add up, so the average repair bill is higher than it was a few years ago.

### Vehicle theft

Theft has improved but is still costly. [Équité Association](https://www.equiteassociation.com/press-releases/equite-associations-2025-auto-theft-trend-report-shows-that-despite-the-ongoing-decline-in-theft-rates-canadians-continue-to-bear-900-million-in-costs-annually) reported that private passenger vehicle thefts fell 18% nationally in 2025. Ontario fell 22% and Quebec fell 25%. Even so, Équité estimates theft still costs Canadians about $900 million a year. Only about half of stolen vehicles in Ontario and Quebec were recovered.

If you drive a model that thieves target, your comprehensive premium can stay high even as theft declines.

### Injury and claim costs

Injury claims are often the most expensive part of auto insurance. Medical care, rehabilitation, income support and legal costs all feed into rates. This is one reason several provinces have changed their injury systems in recent years.

### Severe weather

Hail, flooding and storms damage vehicles as well as homes. The [Insurance Bureau of Canada](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/severe-weather-related-insured-losses-in-canada-exceed-2-4-billion-in-2025) reported more than $2.4 billion in insured severe-weather losses in 2025. IBC also reported about $37 billion in severe-weather losses from 2016 to 2025, compared with about $14 billion in the decade before. Weather damage to cars is paid under comprehensive coverage, so it shows up in that part of your premium.

## How much are premiums rising in your province?

The Applied Rating Index tracks average quoted premiums across a large volume of quotes each quarter. Its second-quarter 2026 results showed very different trends by region.

| Region | Change in average quoted auto premium (Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025) |
|---|---|
| Alberta | Up 22.6%. |
| Atlantic Canada | Up 9.4%. |
| Ontario | Up 7.0%. |
| Quebec | Up 0.9%. |

Source: [Applied Rating Index, Q2 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/22/3331426/0/en/applied-rating-index-q2-2026-results-released.html). Your own change can be higher or lower.

Rules in each province also shape what you pay:

- **Ontario:** Since July 1, 2026, many accident benefits are optional. Dropping them lowers premiums but reduces protection. See our [Ontario auto insurance changes guide](/guides/ontario-auto-insurance-changes-july-2026/).
- **Alberta:** A 7.5% rate cap protects eligible good drivers in 2026. The Care-First model starts January 1, 2027. See our [Alberta car insurance guide](/guides/alberta-car-insurance-2026-care-first/).
- **British Columbia:** ICBC basic rates are frozen through 2027. Optional coverage is where prices vary. See [ICBC optional insurance explained](/guides/icbc-optional-insurance-explained/).
- **Quebec:** The SAAQ covers bodily injury. Private insurers cover liability and vehicle damage.
- **Manitoba and Saskatchewan:** Basic coverage comes from public insurers, MPI and SGI.

## Why did my premium go up when nothing changed?

Sometimes something did change, even if it doesn't feel that way. Common reasons include:

- Your insurer filed a rate increase for your area or vehicle type.
- A discount ended, such as a new-customer or conviction-free discount.
- A ticket or at-fault claim was added to your record.
- You added a driver, especially a new or young driver.
- You moved, changed vehicles or started commuting further.
- Your accident forgiveness was used up.

Ask your insurer or broker for a plain explanation of the increase. You are entitled to understand what you are paying for.

### Questions to ask about your renewal

Use these questions when you call your insurer or broker:

- Which part of my premium went up: liability, accident benefits, collision or comprehensive?
- Did the rate for my area or my vehicle model change?
- Which discounts am I getting now, and did any end?
- Are my vehicle use, commute distance and annual kilometres listed correctly?
- Do your records show any tickets or claims I don't recognize?
- What would I save with a higher deductible?

Errors happen. A wrong address, an old ticket that should have dropped off or the wrong vehicle use can all inflate a premium. Checking the details takes a few minutes.

## 10 practical ways to lower your car insurance premium

1. **Compare quotes at every renewal.** Insurers price the same driver differently. A broker can check several insurers at once.
2. **Bundle your policies.** Many insurers discount auto when you add [home insurance](/home-insurance/) or [tenant insurance](/tenant-insurance/).
3. **Raise your deductibles.** A higher collision or comprehensive deductible lowers your premium. Pick an amount you could pay tomorrow.
4. **Try usage-based insurance.** Telematics programs track driving habits and may reward safe driving. Read how the data is used before you enrol.
5. **Install winter tires.** Many insurers offer a discount. In Ontario, insurers must offer one.
6. **Review coverage on older vehicles.** If your car's value is low, collision coverage may cost more than it's worth to you. Run the numbers with your broker.
7. **Report your kilometres accurately.** Remote work or a shorter commute can lower your rate. In BC, ask about ICBC's low-kilometre discount.
8. **Add anti-theft protection.** An immobilizer or tracking device may earn a discount. Some insurers require one for high-theft models.
9. **Ask about every discount.** Examples include multi-vehicle, claims-free, alumni, employer and professional group discounts.
10. **Protect your record.** Avoid tickets and drive defensively. Before filing a small claim, ask how it would affect your premium. You may still need to report the accident.

> **Tip:** Ask whether paying monthly costs more than paying annually. Some insurers add a fee or interest for monthly payments.

Province rules can help too. In Ontario, review optional accident benefits carefully rather than dropping them all. In Alberta, check whether you qualify for the good-driver rate cap.
