Why is home insurance going up? In Canada, three forces explain most of it: costlier severe weather, higher rebuilding costs and frequent water claims. Insurers are paying more for claims, and premiums follow. In Q2 2026, quoted property premiums were 5.8% higher than a year earlier nationally, and 11.9% higher in Alberta.

## Why is home insurance going up? The short answer

Home insurance is going up because claims cost more. Severe weather losses from hail, wildfire, flood and ice storms hit a record in 2024. Rebuilding a home costs more than it did before 2020, and water damage keeps claims coming. The Applied Rating Index shows quoted property premiums up 5.8% nationally in Q2 2026, year over year.

The long view tells the same story. [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026007-eng.htm) found that prices for homeowners' home and mortgage insurance rose 45.0% from December 2019 to December 2025. Over the same six years, the all-items Consumer Price Index rose 21.0%. Home insurance rose more than twice as fast as prices in general.

Statistics Canada names extreme weather claims as a major driver. It also points to house prices, replacement costs, building input prices and wages. And its study found that home insurance is more affected by extreme weather claims than car insurance.

## Catastrophe losses: hail, wildfire, flood and ice storms

The Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC) reports each year's insured severe-weather losses in January, using figures from Catastrophe Indices and Quantification Inc. (CatIQ).

| Year | Insured severe-weather losses (IBC's first estimate) | How IBC described the year |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | More than $3.1 billion | Fourth-worst year for insured losses at the time |
| 2024 | More than $8.5 billion | Costliest year on record |
| 2025 | More than $2.4 billion | Tenth costliest year on record |

*First estimates from IBC's January releases for [2023](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/severe-weather-in-2023-caused-over-3-1-billion-in-insured-damage), [2024](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/2024-shatters-record-for-costliest-year-for-severe-weather-related-losses-in-canadian-history-at-8-5-billion) and [2025](https://www.ibc.ca/news-insights/news/severe-weather-related-insured-losses-in-canada-exceed-2-4-billion-in-2025), based on CatIQ data. CatIQ revises its figures as claims settle, and IBC's later totals for some years are higher.*

Statistics Canada counts four major catastrophic events within 30 days in the summer of 2024: the Calgary hailstorm, the Jasper wildfire, and flooding in Quebec and Ontario. It also found that every year from 2020 to 2025 ranked among the 10 costliest years on record for extreme weather claims.

2025 was calmer but still expensive, with the late-March ice storm in Ontario and Quebec and the May wildfires in Flin Flon, Manitoba. Over the decade from 2016 to 2025, IBC says insured losses from catastrophic weather and wildfires were nearly triple those of the decade before.

Why would a storm across town raise your premium? Insurers set rates on the claims they expect from similar homes in your area. A run of costly storms nearby can lift your price even if your house came through untouched. In Alberta, hail is a big part of that; our [Calgary hail insurance guide](/guides/calgary-hail-insurance-guide/) covers roof claims and hail deductibles.

## Rebuilding, materials and labour costs

House insurance pays to rebuild your home, not what it would sell for. When labour and materials cost more, every claim costs more, and so does the coverage you need.

Statistics Canada's June 2026 infographic on [what's driving premiums higher](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-627-m/11-627-m2026026-eng.htm) points to construction costs, tariffs and labour shortages. Residential construction costs climbed much faster than general inflation after 2019. Recent data shows that pressure easing, but not gone:

- The [building construction price index](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260724/dq260724b-eng.htm) shows residential construction costs up 2.3% year over year in Q2 2026. Metal and steel products led, which Statistics Canada ties to retaliatory tariffs and supply chain disruption.
- In that quarter, Calgary, Vancouver and Toronto were the only metropolitan areas where those costs fell from the previous quarter.
- The Consumer Price Index measure of homeowners' replacement cost was down 2.5% year over year in [May 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260622/dq260622a-eng.htm), its 13th straight month of year-over-year decline.

Your renewal can still rise if your insurer updates the rebuild cost on your policy. Here's how that one change moves Instasure's Ontario example.

| Insured rebuild cost | Typical monthly estimate | Typical annual estimate |
|---|---|---|
| $500,000 (example profile) | $133 | $1,600 |
| $550,000 (10% higher) | $143 | $1,720 |
| $600,000 (20% higher) | $153 | $1,830 |

*Instasure estimate for an example profile (Ontario house built in 2000, $1,000 deductible, $2 million liability, sewer backup and overland water endorsements, no past claims), as of October 2026. Modelled figures, not quotes and not averages.*

Don't lower your rebuild cost just to save money: set too low, it could leave you short after a total loss. Ask your insurer or a licensed advisor how the figure was calculated.

## Water damage and sewer backup claims

Water is another way climate change shows up in home insurance in Canada. [IBC](https://www.ibc.ca/stay-protected/severe-weather-centre/flooding-and-insurance) says warmer temperatures are bringing short, high-intensity rainfalls, which lead to more frequent and severe flooding.

How your policy treats water depends on where it comes from:

- **Sudden leaks inside the home** from plumbing, heating or air conditioning are typically covered by a standard policy.
- **Sewer backup** through a floor drain is covered only if you add a sewer backup endorsement.
- **Overland flooding** from heavy rain or rising rivers is optional coverage, priced on your risk, and often sold together with sewer backup.
- **Groundwater and seepage** are typically not standard coverages.

Finished basements make water claims costly. Where many homes file water claims, insurers may raise rates or tighten water limits.

Our guide to [does home insurance cover water damage](/guides/does-home-insurance-cover-water-damage-canada/) explains each type of water claim in detail, including flood insurance in Canada. In Instasure's Ontario example, removing the water endorsements lowers the estimate from $133 to $120 a month. That saves $13 a month but leaves sewer backup and overland water uninsured.

### Alberta

Alberta tops both columns. Statistics Canada found Alberta had the largest cumulative increase in homeowners' insurance prices of any province: 391.6% from December 2005 to December 2025. It links this partly to hailstorms, wildfires and convective storms. Our model adds a hail loading in Calgary, which lifts its estimate to $197 a month. ## What you can do at renewal

### Why did my home insurance go up if I made no claims?

A home insurance renewal increase doesn't mean you did something wrong. Common reasons include:

- Your insurer raised rates for your area after regional storm or water losses.
- Your insured rebuild cost was updated.
- A discount ended, such as a new-customer discount.
- You changed the home, for example by finishing the basement, adding a suite or starting a home business.

### Renewal-notice checklist

Use this list as soon as your renewal notice arrives.

1. **Compare line by line.** Put last year's and this year's premium side by side for the dwelling, contents, liability and each endorsement.
2. **Check the rebuild cost.** Did it change, and does it match your home's size, finishes and renovations?
3. **Review the deductible.** In Instasure's Ontario example, a $2,500 deductible instead of $1,000 lowers the estimate from $133 to $117 a month, and $5,000 brings it to $107. Choose an amount your savings can cover.
4. **Confirm your claims record.** One past claim raises the same example from $133 to $167 a month, so make sure your record is accurate.
5. **Keep water coverage.** Check your sewer backup and overland water limits rather than dropping them.
6. **Ask about discounts,** such as bundling your home and car policies.
7. **Ask about upgrades.** A newer roof, updated plumbing or electrical, or a backwater valve may change how an insurer rates your home. Ask before you spend.
8. **Ask why it went up.** Your insurer should be able to tell you which part of the premium changed.
9. **Get a fresh estimate** and compare it with your renewal before you let the policy renew automatically.

*Instasure estimates for the Ontario example profile above, as of October 2026. Real insurers weigh these factors differently.*

A licensed advisor can compare several insurers at once. It's worth asking for help when:

- Your increase is well above the regional figure in the table.
- Your insurer declined to renew or added a large water or hail deductible. Start with our [hard-to-insure home desk](/home-insurance/hard-to-insure/).
- You rent your home short-term or own a cottage. See the [short-term rental desk](/home-insurance/short-term-rental/) or the [cottage and rural property desk](/home-insurance/cottage-and-seasonal/).
- Your car premium jumped too. Our guide on [why your car insurance is going up](/guides/why-is-my-car-insurance-going-up-2026/) covers that side, and bundling may help both.

When you compare, keep the rebuild cost, deductible, liability limit and water coverage the same. Renewal is usually the simplest time to switch. Ask your current insurer whether it charges a fee if you cancel partway through the term.

## Michael's take

As a planner, I don't try to predict next year's home insurance rate, and I'd be wary of anyone who does. What I do look at is the trend. Statistics Canada's figures show home insurance prices rising more than twice as fast as general prices since 2019. My rule of thumb is to budget for the renewal to keep outpacing the rest of the household budget. If it comes in lower, that money goes to savings.

When a renewal jumps, I'd look for savings in the deductible before the coverage. If your emergency fund can absorb a $2,500 deductible, the model shows a real saving. Cutting water endorsements or the rebuild cost to shave a few dollars a month leaves your plan exposed to the loss that could derail it. And money spent on prevention, like a sound roof or a backwater valve, protects your home and your claims record at the same time.
