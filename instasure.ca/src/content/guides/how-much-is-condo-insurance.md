How much is condo insurance? For one example owner, Instasure's model puts the condo insurance cost at about $32 to $42 a month in eight major Canadian cities, as of October 2026. Your own price depends on your city, how much you insure and the deductibles in your building's policy. Below you'll find dated tables by city and coverage level, and the levers that change your premium.

## What is the typical condo insurance cost in Canada?

For an example owner with $50,000 of contents, $25,000 of improvements, $50,000 of deductible-assessment coverage, $2 million liability and a $1,000 deductible, Instasure's model estimates a typical condo insurance cost of $32 to $42 a month across eight major cities. That's about $384 to $504 a year, as of October 2026.

A condo unit policy is smaller than a house policy because it doesn't insure the building. The [Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC)](https://www.ibc.ca/insurance-basics/home/types-of-home-insurance-coverage) explains that the corporation's policy covers the building and common property. Your unit policy typically covers:

- **Your belongings**, such as furniture, clothing and electronics.
- **Improvements** made by you or previous owners, up to a stated limit.
- **Personal liability** if you injure someone or damage their property.
- **Additional living expenses** if you have to move out after an insured loss.

IBC lists optional add-ons too: extra improvements coverage, loss assessment, contingency coverage for gaps in the corporation's insurance, earthquake, sewer backup and overland flood.

That's why the gap with a house is so wide. Instasure's Toronto house example, with a $500,000 rebuild cost, is $139 a month. The Toronto condo example is $39. The building's insurance is still a cost you share, through your condo fees.

## Why Toronto and Vancouver differ

In our model, [condo insurance in Vancouver](/condo-insurance/british-columbia/vancouver/) is $42 a month and Toronto is $39 for the same unit. Vancouver matches the BC provincial estimate, which our model sets higher than Ontario's $38. Toronto sits $1 above Ontario because of the city's flood loading.

The bigger difference is the risk you carry as an owner. In BC, the [provincial government](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/finances-and-insurance/strata-owner-and-tenant-insurance) says strata corporation insurance deductibles can range from $100,000 to $750,000 or higher. It also says:

- A strata corporation can recover its deductible from an owner who is responsible for the loss.
- The owner doesn't have to be negligent to be held responsible. A failed dishwasher hose is the kind of case it describes.
- Strata owner insurance can cover some or all of the cost of the strata's deductible.

Since April 1, 2023, the [Form B information certificate](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2023HOUS0007-000081) must include a summary of the strata's insurance coverage. If you're buying, read it before you choose your limits. Earthquake coverage is a separate add-on, so ask about it directly. Our [condo insurance in BC](/condo-insurance/british-columbia/) page has provincial figures.

In Toronto's high-rise buildings, water from one unit can reach the units below. Ontario's chargeback rules, covered in the next section, decide when that becomes your bill.

## Deductible-assessment limits and price

Deductible-assessment coverage pays when your corporation charges its deductible back to you. After a water leak, it can be the line on your policy that matters most.

In Ontario, the [Condominium Authority of Ontario](https://www.condoauthorityontario.ca/knowledge-base/what-does-condo-insurance-cover/) explains that under section 105 of the Condominium Act, an owner whose act or omission causes damage can be charged the lesser of the deductible or the actual repair cost. A corporation can extend this by by-law, including to accidental damage where no one is at fault. The corporation insures the standard unit set out in its governing documents. Upgrades beyond it are improvements you insure yourself.

The CAO also notes that owners aren't required by law to carry insurance. Your corporation's governing documents may still require it.

On price, our model holds deductible assessment at $50,000 and doesn't price the limit on its own. Its comprehensive + water tier, which includes a higher deductible-assessment limit, costs about 18% more than standard comprehensive. That's $46 against $39 a month in Toronto. Real insurers price each limit their own way, so ask for quotes at two or three limits.

To choose a limit, start with the largest deductible in your building's policy, and check the water-damage deductible in particular. Our guide to [condo insurance deductible coverage](/guides/condo-insurance-deductible-assessment-explained/) walks through the steps. It also explains the [strata insurance deductible in BC](/guides/condo-insurance-deductible-assessment-explained/) and how loss assessment differs.

## Are condo insurance prices going up?

For the broader trend, Statistics Canada's study released June 16, 2026, tracked homeowners' insurance. Homeowners' home and mortgage insurance prices rose 45.0% from December 2019 to December 2025. Over the same period, overall consumer prices rose 21.0%.

The study found 2024 was the costliest year on record for extreme weather claims. It named the Calgary hailstorm, the Jasper wildfire and flooding in Ontario and Quebec in the third quarter of 2024 as significant contributors. Condo buildings face the same weather. When the corporation's costs and deductibles rise, more risk can shift onto owners through chargebacks and assessments.

## Ways to lower your condo premium

These levers come from the [Insurance Bureau of Canada](https://www.ibc.ca/insurance-basics/home/how-to-drive-down-your-home-insurance-rates) and from how our model moves:

1. **Insure what you own, not a round number.** A room-by-room inventory sets your contents limit at what you really own. In our Toronto example, $35,000 of contents and improvements is $26 a month against $39 for $75,000.
2. **Choose a deductible you can afford.** IBC says a higher deductible lowers the premium, but make sure you could pay it after a loss.
3. **Bundle with your car insurance.** IBC notes that insurers may offer a multi-policy discount when you combine home and car coverage.
4. **Tell your insurer about protective devices.** IBC lists burglar alarms and sprinkler systems among devices that can reduce premiums.
5. **Save claims for real losses.** In our model, one past claim raises the Toronto estimate from $39 to $47 a month.
6. **Shop around at renewal.** IBC calls this the simplest way to save.

What not to cut: your deductible-assessment limit. Below your building's largest deductible, you pay the difference yourself, and BC strata deductibles can reach six figures.

## Michael's take

As a planner, I look at condo insurance as one of the smaller lines in an owner's budget, set against the home it protects. The premium differences in these tables are a few dollars a month. The difference between having the right deductible-assessment limit and not having it can be tens of thousands of dollars. My rule of thumb is to size that limit to your building's largest deductible first, then fit the rest of the policy around your budget.

I'd also tie your condo insurance to your emergency fund. Your own deductible should be an amount you could pay the week after a leak without touching your retirement savings. And review the policy every time your corporation renews its insurance, because the building's deductibles can change even when your premium doesn't.
