The CPP death benefit is a one-time payment of $2,500 to the estate of a person who contributed enough to the Canada Pension Plan. Since January 1, 2025, some estates also get a $2,500 top-up, for a maximum of $5,000. Below you'll find who qualifies, who applies and how, and how much of a funeral the benefit really covers.

## How much is the CPP death benefit?

The CPP death benefit is a one-time, taxable payment of $2,500 when the person who died made enough contributions to the Canada Pension Plan. For deaths on or after January 1, 2025, a $2,500 top-up can apply, for a maximum of $5,000. The amount can be lower if a social security agreement is needed to qualify.

These figures come from the [Government of Canada's death benefit page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/cpp-death-benefit.html). The basic amount is the same for every eligible contributor, whatever they earned.

The death benefit is separate from the monthly CPP survivor's pension and the children's benefit. Family members apply for those on their own forms.

## Who qualifies: CPP contribution rules

The person who died must have contributed to the CPP for at least:

- **one-third of the calendar years in their contributory period** for the base CPP, and no fewer than three calendar years; **or**
- **10 calendar years.**

The [contributory period](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html) generally starts at age 18. It ends at the earliest of death, the start of a CPP retirement pension, or age 70. In practice, anyone with 10 calendar years of contributions meets the test. A younger person can qualify with fewer years, but never fewer than three.

If the person also paid into the Quebec Pension Plan, contributions to both plans are combined. If they lived or worked in a country that has a social security agreement with Canada, that can help them qualify. In that case, the payment can be lower.

### Qualification checklist

| Question | Basic $2,500 | $2,500 top-up |
|---|---|---|
| Did the person meet the contribution test above? | Must be yes | Must be yes |
| Did they die on or after January 1, 2025? | Not a condition | Must be yes |
| Did they ever receive a CPP or QPP retirement pension? | Not a condition | Must be no |
| Did they ever receive a CPP or QPP disability benefit or post-retirement disability benefit? | Not a condition | Must be no |
| Did they leave a spouse or common-law partner eligible for a CPP survivor's pension? | Not a condition | Must be no |

*Rules from canada.ca, as of October 2026. Service Canada decides eligibility from the person's contribution record.*

## The $2,500 CPP death benefit top-up since January 2025

The top-up was one of several CPP changes that took effect on January 1, 2025, according to the [federal backgrounder on the changes](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/news/2025/01/backgrounder-changes-to-the-canada-pension-plan.html). It's for contributors who die before collecting a CPP pension and who leave no spouse or partner eligible for a survivor's pension. Their estates can receive $5,000 instead of $2,500.

Two examples show how the rules play out:

- **A single 40-year-old** has contributed for 15 years, never drew a CPP benefit and has no partner. If they die, their estate can receive the basic $2,500 plus the $2,500 top-up: $5,000 in all.
- **A 72-year-old retiree** has been receiving a CPP retirement pension. If they met the contribution test, their estate can receive the basic $2,500, but not the top-up, whether or not they had a spouse.

So if you already receive your CPP retirement pension and you're planning your own funeral, plan on no more than $2,500.

## How to apply for the CPP death benefit

### Who can apply

If there is an estate, the executor named in the will applies, or the administrator named by the court. Service Canada says the executor should apply within 60 days of the date of death.

If there's no estate, or the executor hasn't applied, the benefit can be paid to others who apply. The order of priority is:

1. The person or institution that paid, or is responsible for paying, the funeral expenses.
2. The surviving spouse or common-law partner.
3. The next of kin.

### Steps to apply

1. **Online:** sign in to My Service Canada Account and complete the CPP Death Benefit form. You can upload supporting documents if they're needed.
2. **On paper:** complete form ISP1200, the Application for a Canada Pension Plan Death Benefit. Mail it with copies of the documents it lists, and write the deceased's Social Insurance Number and your own on every document.
3. **Keep the originals safe.** Copies are generally accepted, but Service Canada can ask for an original or a certified copy at any time.
4. **Apply for survivor benefits as well.** A spouse or partner may qualify for the monthly survivor's pension, and children may qualify for the children's benefit. The [survivor's pension page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/cpp-survivor-pension.html) advises applying as soon as possible. Back payments are limited to 12 months: 11 months plus the month you apply.
5. **Quebec:** contact [Retraite Québec](https://www.retraitequebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/about-us/survivors-benefits/death-benefit-quebec-pension-plan) instead if the person contributed only to the QPP or lived in Quebec at the time of death. The same applies if they lived outside Canada and Quebec was their last province.

### Is the CPP death benefit taxable?

Yes. The [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/life-events/doing-taxes-someone-died/prepare-returns/report-income/death-benefits.html) says it's taxable and reported on a T4A(P) slip. It's generally taxed as income of the estate, or of the person who received it. It doesn't go on the deceased's final return.

It doesn't qualify for the $10,000 death benefit exemption that applies to some employer death benefits, so the amount left after tax can be less than $2,500. For your own return, follow the CRA's guidance or ask a tax professional.

## Does the CPP death benefit cover a funeral?

It helps, but whether it covers the funeral depends on the funeral you choose. There's no official national figure for the cost of a funeral in Canada. Each funeral home sets its own prices, and the total depends on your choices: burial or cremation, a casket or urn, embalming, a visitation, the service, the cemetery and the reception.

### Funeral costs in Canada: start with the price list

Funerals are regulated by the provinces and territories, and their rules make prices easier to compare:

- **Ontario:** licensed operators must give you a full, current price list. Those with a website must also [post their price list online](https://thebao.ca/notice-post-your-price-list-licence-and-link-to-the-bao-consumer-information-guide-on-your-websites/), under rules from the Bereavement Authority of Ontario.
- **British Columbia:** [Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/consumer-help/consumer-information-funeral-rights/) says funeral providers must display a current price list and give a copy to anyone who asks.
- **Everywhere:** the federal [Office of Consumer Affairs](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/office-consumer-affairs/en/modern-marketplace/funerals) suggests reviewing a package and removing the services you don't need.

### A worked example: the funeral funding gap

Ask two or three funeral homes near you for their price lists, and price the funeral you actually want. Then subtract the $2,500 CPP benefit. The table uses round example budgets, not averages, so you can see how the gap grows.

| Example funeral budget | Basic CPP benefit | Gap with the basic benefit | Gap if the top-up applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| $5,000 | $2,500 | $2,500 | $0 |
| $10,000 | $2,500 | $7,500 | $5,000 |
| $15,000 | $2,500 | $12,500 | $10,000 |
| $20,000 | $2,500 | $17,500 | $15,000 |

*Example budgets for illustration only; use the totals from your own price lists. Gaps are before tax on the CPP benefit, which can make the real gap larger.*

Timing matters too. The benefit arrives only after someone applies and Service Canada processes the claim. So the family may need to pay the funeral home before any CPP money comes in.

## Closing the gap: savings, funeral insurance or a pre-arranged plan

There are three common ways to fund the difference, and you can combine them.

### 1. Savings set aside for the funeral

A savings account or TFSA earmarked for the funeral stays flexible. The risks are that it gets spent on something else, or that you die before you've saved enough.

### 2. Funeral insurance

[Funeral insurance](/life-insurance/funeral-expense/) is a small permanent [life insurance](/life-insurance/) policy. It pays a lump sum to the person you name, who can use it with any funeral home or for other bills. Instasure's estimates cover policies from $5,000 to $50,000.

Who you name matters. The [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/life.html) explains that if your estate is the beneficiary, the payout becomes part of the estate. Creditors may then claim it. If you name no one, the insurer pays your estate by default.

Here is what a policy could cost, by age and amount:

| Example profile (non-smoker) | $5,000 | $10,000 | $15,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woman, age 50 | $16.50/mo | $29.50/mo | $42/mo |
| Man, age 50 | $19.50/mo | $35/mo | $50.50/mo |
| Woman, age 60 | $24.50/mo | $45/mo | $65.50/mo |
| Man, age 60 | $29/mo | $54/mo | $79/mo |
| Woman, age 70 | $40.50/mo | $77/mo | $113.50/mo |
| Man, age 70 | $48.50/mo | $93/mo | $137.50/mo |

*Instasure estimates for example profiles: non-smoker, simplified-issue funeral expense policy, level premiums for life, typical monthly premium, as of October 2026. These are modelled estimates, not quotes or averages; insurers set real prices after underwriting.*

For the 50-year-old woman, $10,000 of cover at $29.50 a month works out to about $0.97 a day. At that rate, she would pay $10,000 in total premiums after about 28 years. The 70-year-old man at $93 a month would reach $10,000 in about nine years.

Two checks before you buy. First, compare the total premiums you're likely to pay with the benefit. Second, if a plan asks no health questions, check whether it limits the payout in the first years.

If you want room for final bills and debts as well as the funeral, compare [final expense insurance](/life-insurance/final-expense/). For bigger needs, such as income for a partner, see [life insurance for seniors](/life-insurance/seniors/) or [whole life insurance](/whole-life-insurance/).

### 3. A pre-arranged funeral contract

A pre-arranged (prepaid) contract buys set services from a funeral home, under your province's rules. In Ontario, the [Bereavement Authority of Ontario](https://thebao.ca/pre-plan-prepay/) says you get a full refund if your money is held in trust and you cancel within 30 days of signing. After that, the provider may keep 10% of what you paid, to a maximum of $350. It must still refund the income earned on the money. Cancelling a prepaid contract doesn't necessarily cancel a related insurance policy.

The federal guide for older Canadians on [wills and funeral plans](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/corporate/seniors-forum-federal-provincial-territorial/will-funeral-plan.html) gives a simple test. Don't prepay unless you can get your money back if you move, change your mind or the funeral home goes out of business.

## Michael's take

As a planner, I treat the CPP death benefit as a helpful cheque, not a funeral plan. For anyone already drawing a CPP retirement pension, it's $2,500 at most, it's taxable, and it only arrives after someone applies. I'd leave the top-up out of your numbers unless you're sure every condition fits.

My rule of thumb is to price the funeral you actually want from a local price list, subtract $2,500 and fund the gap on purpose. Savings work if you can leave them alone. A small policy makes sense if you'd rather lock in a level premium, but compare the total premiums with the benefit first. Whatever you choose, tell your executor where the plan is, so the 60-day window and the survivor benefits aren't missed.
