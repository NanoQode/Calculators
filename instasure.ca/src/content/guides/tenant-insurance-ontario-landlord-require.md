Yes, Ontario landlords can require tenant insurance as a condition of the lease, and the province's standard lease has a section for it. No law makes every renter buy it, but if your lease requires it, you must keep coverage in force and show proof when asked. This guide explains how tenant insurance Ontario renters buy works, what it covers, what it costs and what students should know.

## Can a landlord require tenant insurance in Ontario?

Yes. Most private residential tenancies in Ontario must use the province's standard form of lease. It includes a section on tenant's insurance. In that section, the landlord indicates whether the tenant must carry liability insurance.

If the landlord requires it, the tenant must keep liability coverage in place and provide proof when asked. Contents insurance for your own belongings is left up to you. The Government of Ontario's [guide to the standard lease](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-ontarios-standard-lease) explains each section.

Some leases name a minimum liability amount. Others just say "liability insurance." Read the clause closely so you buy the amount the lease asks for.

What if you don't have it? The answer isn't set by the lease alone. Whether a tenancy can end over missing insurance depends on the Residential Tenancies Act and decisions of the [Landlord and Tenant Board](https://tribunalsontario.ca/ltb/). Don't test it. Buy the coverage, keep proof on file and renew on time.

> **Tip:** Read the tenant's insurance section of your lease before you sign. If the landlord wants proof, ask your insurer for a certificate or a copy of the declarations page on the day you buy.

## Why your landlord's policy doesn't protect you

Many renters assume the landlord's insurance covers them. It doesn't. A landlord's policy protects the building and the landlord's own liability.

If a fire damages your unit, the landlord's insurer repairs the walls. It won't replace your laptop, pay for your hotel or defend you if the fire started in your kitchen. Those risks fall on you unless you have your own policy.

## What does tenant insurance cover?

A tenant policy bundles several coverages into one price. Here is what a typical package includes.

| Coverage | What it pays for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contents | Your belongings, at home and often away from home. | A break-in where your laptop and bike are stolen. |
| Additional living expenses | Extra costs if you can't live in your unit after an insured loss. | Hotel and meals while smoke damage is repaired. |
| Personal liability | Claims if you injure someone or damage their property. | A guest slips and is hurt in your apartment. |
| Tenant's legal liability | Damage you cause to the landlord's property. | A kitchen fire you started damages the unit. |
| Voluntary medical payments | Small medical costs for an injured guest. | A visitor's physiotherapy after a fall. |

Your overflowing bathtub that leaks into the unit below is a classic liability claim. Without a policy, you could be paying those repair costs yourself.

Common add-ons include sewer backup, overland water, identity theft, and higher limits for jewellery, bikes or cameras. Our [water damage guide](/guides/does-home-insurance-cover-water-damage-canada/) explains why water add-ons matter, even in an apartment.

Two choices shape every policy:

- **Named perils vs comprehensive:** A named-perils policy covers only listed causes of loss. A comprehensive policy covers all causes except listed exclusions.
- **Replacement cost vs actual cash value:** Replacement cost pays to buy new items. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation, so you get less for older items.

| $30,000 | About $20 to $33 |

*Instasure model, example profile: Ontario renter, $2 million liability, $1,000 deductible, no recent claims. These are illustrative ranges, not quotes. Prices vary by insurer, address, building and claims history.*

Most tenant insurance Ontario renters buy is priced on these factors:

- Your postal code and local claims history.
- The building's age, construction and fire protection.
- How much contents coverage you choose.
- Your deductible.
- Your past claims.
- Whether you bundle with car insurance.

## Tenant insurance for students

Students often have more options than they realize, and more gaps.

**Parents' home policy.** Some parents' home policies extend limited coverage to a full-time student living away at school. The age limits, contents limits and liability terms vary widely. Ask the parents' insurer in writing what is covered, and whether that meets the lease.

**Off-campus rentals with roommates.** Each roommate usually needs a separate policy. Insurers often won't cover unrelated people under one policy. One roommate's policy won't automatically protect the others' belongings or liability.

**Residence.** Some university residences recommend or require coverage. Check your residence agreement.

Student cities like [Ottawa](/tenant-insurance/ontario/ottawa/) and [Toronto](/tenant-insurance/ontario/toronto/) have many shared rentals, so get proof sorted before move-in day.

## Should you buy it if your lease doesn't require it?

In most cases, yes. The lease requirement protects the landlord. The policy itself protects you.

Think about what it would cost to replace everything you own at once: furniture, clothes, electronics, kitchen items. The total often adds up faster than you'd expect. Then add a week or two in a hotel after a fire or a burst pipe.

Liability is the bigger risk. If a fire starts in your unit and spreads, the claims against you could be far larger than the value of your belongings. A tenant policy pays for your legal defence and covered damages, up to your limit.

If money is tight, choose a higher deductible and a realistic contents amount rather than going without coverage.

## How to show proof to your landlord

Most landlords accept one of these documents:

1. A certificate or proof of insurance from your insurer.
2. The declarations page showing your name, address, dates and liability limit.

Ask your insurer to add the landlord as an "additional interest." This usually means the landlord is told if your policy is cancelled. Some landlords instead ask to be an "additional insured," which is different. Ask your insurer what it can offer.

Keep proof current. Send updated documents after each renewal, and tell your insurer when you move.

> **Tip:** Set a calendar reminder two weeks before renewal. A lapse can breach your lease, and it leaves you uninsured.
