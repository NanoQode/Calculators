Simplified vs guaranteed issue life insurance comes down to one trade-off: how much the insurer learns about your health, and what you give up in return. Fewer questions mean less cover, a higher price and, for guaranteed issue, a two-year wait for the full benefit. A third route, accelerated underwriting, skips the medical exam for healthy applicants at regular rates.

Below you'll find a five-question decision tree, a dated comparison table and Instasure estimates for the same example profiles.

## What is the difference between simplified vs guaranteed issue life insurance?

Simplified issue asks a few yes-or-no health questions, then pays the full benefit once you're approved; the plans we checked go up to $75,000 or $100,000. Guaranteed issue asks no health questions, caps cover at $25,000 or $40,000, and usually withholds the full benefit for a death from illness in the first two years.

Accelerated underwriting is different again. You answer the full application, but a healthy applicant skips the exam and buys the insurer's regular policy. All three are "no-medical" in the sense that nobody takes blood. What changes is how much the insurer knows, and that sets the price and the limits.

## Which no-medical option you may qualify for: a five-question decision tree

Work through these questions in order and stop at the first route that fits. It's a guide to where to start, not an underwriting decision.

1. **Do you need more than $100,000 of cover?** If you're protecting a mortgage or replacing an income, the answer is usually yes. Go to question 2. If you only need a smaller amount, such as for final expenses, go to question 3.
2. **Are you 60 or younger, in good health, with no pending tests or recent hospital stays?** If yes, start with **accelerated underwriting**. You may skip the exam and pay regular rates. If no, a fully underwritten application, with an exam if the insurer asks, is still usually the way to get a large amount. Talk to our medical underwriting desk before you apply.
3. **Can you answer "no" to short health questions about the last few years?** Typical questions ask about your health over the last five years, pending tests or referrals, and recent hospital stays. If yes, start with **simplified issue**.
4. **Is a past condition now stable and treated?** If yes, ask a licensed advisor about a **deferred or graded plan**, which may accept you with a reduced benefit in the early years.
5. **Are you between 30 and 75, and is $25,000 to $40,000 enough?** If yes, **guaranteed issue** will accept you within each plan's age limits (Sun Life 30 to 74, RBC 40 to 75), with a two-year limit on the payout. If no, compare it with setting money aside in savings.

## Accelerated underwriting: no exam at regular prices

Accelerated underwriting isn't a separate product. It's a faster way to underwrite an insurer's regular term or permanent policy. You answer the full set of health and lifestyle questions. The insurer then checks your answers against data sources instead of sending a nurse.

Two examples show how it works in Canada:

- **BMO Insurance** announced in [February 2024](https://newsroom.bmo.com/2024-02-26-BMO-Insurance-Enhances-its-Accelerated-Underwriting-and-Simplifies-Requirements-for-New-Life-Insurance-Coverage) that eligible applicants could be underwritten without further medical tests up to $5 million at ages 18 to 40. The limits are $3 million at ages 41 to 50 and $750,000 at ages 51 to 60. On [July 9, 2026](https://newsroom.bmo.com/2026-07-09-BMO-Insurance-launches-AI-enhanced-SmartDecision-tool-delivering-a-faster-and-more-seamless-path-to-coverage-for-clients-and-advisors), BMO launched SmartDecision. It gives real-time decisions on eligible applications up to $5 million across its term, universal life and whole life products, and eligible applicants don't need tele-interviews or medical evidence. It's available through advisors.
- **Sun Life** says healthy applicants aged 40 or under can generally skip the exam for [Sun Life Go Term](https://www.sunlife.ca/en/insurance/life/term-life-insurance/sun-life-go/). Healthy applicants aged 41 to 50 can skip it for up to $500,000. An exam is still needed if you answer "yes" to any health question, or if specific information shows up in your Medical Information Bureau (MIB) record.

The [Canadian Life and Health Insurance Association](https://www.clhia.ca/en-ca/consumers/glossary-of-insurance-terms) describes MIB as a non-profit association that lets its member insurers share information confidentially. It helps insurers spot applications with incomplete or false information. So "no exam" never means "no checking".

Because you end up with the insurer's regular policy, you pay that policy's normal rates for your health. That's why accelerated underwriting is the first door to try if you're healthy. For more on regular rates, see our guide to [how much life insurance costs](/guides/how-much-does-life-insurance-cost-canada/).

## Simplified issue: a few health questions

Simplified issue life insurance trades depth for speed. You answer a short list of questions, and the insurer decides on those answers alone.

[Sun Life Go Simplified](https://www.sunlife.ca/en/insurance/life/term-life-insurance/sun-life-go-simplified/) asks three questions:

- your health over the last five years
- any pending medical tests or referrals
- any stay of 48 or more consecutive hours in an emergency room or hospital in the last three years

It offers $50,000, $75,000 or $100,000 to Canadian residents aged 18 to 69. The premium is fixed for the first 10 years. After that, the policy renews each year and the premium rises with your age. Sun Life's comparison page says coverage can continue to age 85 while you keep paying. Sun Life also notes it typically costs more than insurance that asks for more health information.

[Manulife CoverMe Easy Issue](https://www.manulife.ca/personal/insurance/our-products/life-insurance/term-life-insurance/coverme-easy-issue-life.html) asks two medical questions, with no exam. It offers $50,000 or $75,000 on a 10-year term, renewable to age 85. Manulife says you're covered once the application is approved and the first premium is received.

### Deferred plans: the in-between tier

A "yes" to one question can rule you out of a simplified plan. Some insurers offer a deferred plan instead. [Canada Protection Plan's Deferred Elite Term](https://www.cpp.ca/no-medical-deferred-elite-term-insurance/) pays the full amount if death happens after two years or is accidental. For a non-accidental death in the second year, it pays 50%. The same insurer says its Simplified Elite Term gives immediate coverage with no waiting period. The gap between those two plans is what a health answer can cost you.

## Guaranteed issue: no questions, with a waiting period

Guaranteed issue life insurance asks no health questions. Within the plan's age limits, acceptance is guaranteed. The price of that certainty is a smaller amount, a higher premium and a two-year limit on the payout.

[RBC Insurance's Guaranteed Acceptance Life](https://www.rbcinsurance.com/en-ca/life-insurance/guaranteed-life-insurance/) is open to Canadian citizens and permanent residents aged 40 to 75, for $5,000 to $40,000. Premiums are level and payable to age 95, after which coverage continues. RBC's [disclosure](https://www.rbcinsurance.com/en-ca/life-insurance/guaranteed-acceptance-disclosure/) says a death in the first two years is limited to a refund of premiums paid, without interest, unless it is caused by an accident. You get 30 days after receiving the policy to review it and return it for a full refund.

[Sun Life Go Guaranteed](https://www.sunlife.ca/en/insurance/life/permanent-life-insurance/sun-life-go-guaranteed/) is open to Canadian residents aged 30 to 74. It sells $5,000 to $25,000 in $5,000 steps, and total cover is capped at $25,000 even if you apply more than once. [Sun Life's comparison page](https://www.sunlife.ca/en/insurance/life/sun-life-go-compare-life-insurance/) says it won't pay a claim for a death in the first two years from any cause other than an accident. Premiums stay the same to age 95, and coverage lasts for life.

These amounts suit the funeral and final bills, not a mortgage. That's why guaranteed issue usually sits alongside [final expense insurance](/life-insurance/final-expense/) and [funeral insurance](/life-insurance/funeral-expense/). Government help is modest: our guide to the [CPP death benefit](/guides/cpp-death-benefit-and-funeral-costs/) explains what the estate may receive.

In the term table, simplified issue runs about 30% above full underwriting. In the permanent table, guaranteed issue runs about 45% above simplified issue. Take the 60-year-old man. As guaranteed issue, his premiums would equal the $25,000 benefit after about 11 years. As simplified issue, it would take about 16 years. In the first two years he'd pay about $4,489 in guaranteed-issue premiums, and a death from illness in that window would return premiums rather than the benefit.

## Common mistakes on no-medical applications

- **Skipping straight to guaranteed issue.** If you can answer health questions, you're likely paying more than you need to, and waiting two years for the full benefit.
- **Guessing or leaving things out.** The CLHIA says most policies have a two-year contestability period. Withholding a material fact, such as an ongoing medical check, could lead the insurer to cancel the policy and refuse claims. The [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/make-claim.html) also notes an insurer can deny a claim within that period.
- **Buying a small policy for a big need.** A $25,000 plan won't carry a mortgage. Size the need first with our [life insurance calculator](/calculators/life-insurance-needs/), then look at [term life insurance](/term-life-insurance/).
- **Assuming the premium never moves.** Some simplified term plans, such as Sun Life Go Simplified, fix the premium for 10 years and then raise it every year.
- **Stacking guaranteed-issue policies.** Sun Life caps Go Guaranteed at $25,000 in total, however many times you apply.
- **Not using the review period.** Read the policy when it arrives. RBC gives 30 days on its guaranteed-acceptance plan.

## Across Canada, including Quebec

Each insurer sets its own residency rules. RBC's guaranteed plan requires Canadian citizenship or permanent residence, and Sun Life's plans are for Canadian residents. Instasure's licensed advisors serve every province except Quebec and the territories. For Quebec residents, this guide is information only.

If a claim is refused, the FCAC says to start with the insurer's head office. If you're still not satisfied, you can go to the OmbudService for Life and Health Insurance or your provincial regulator. Over 60? See our page on [life insurance for seniors](/life-insurance/seniors/).

## Michael's take

As a planner, I'd always start at the top of the decision tree and work down. Try accelerated underwriting or full underwriting first. Drop to simplified issue only when the health questions stop you. Guaranteed issue is the last door, not the first. Every step down costs more and covers less, and the estimates above show how fast that adds up.

My rule of thumb is to match the route to the job. A mortgage or a young family needs real underwriting and a real amount. A funeral can be covered by a small policy or by savings set aside for it. Be honest on every answer, because a policy that's contested at claim time protects nobody.
